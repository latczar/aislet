It looks it up, then tells you where.

Aislet answers straight away, then checks the libraries on your phone. When one has something, it offers it: tap Answer from it, and the new answer names the article it used, so you can check it against the page. Or have a library used every time.

  • Wikipedia

    The encyclopedia, in full or in smaller selections.

  • WikiMed

    Wikipedia’s medical articles, for health questions.

  • Appropedia

    Practical guides to water, energy, food and building.

  • Any Kiwix library

    Already use Kiwix? Aislet moves your .zim files in without copying them.

Everyday help, done on the device.

Yours to look after, not a service.

  • Learns from you, with Undo

    It picks up facts about you as you talk, and how you like answers when you correct it (“shorter, please”). Each thing it learns shows under the reply with Undo, and Memory lists them all.

  • Phone and laptop together

    Pair them by scanning a QR code, with no account. At home the phone uses the computer’s AI, and memory and notes stay in sync.

  • Remembers earlier chats

    Ask “what did we say about…?” and it looks back through earlier chats on that device and says roughly when. If it finds nothing, it says so rather than guessing.

  • Encrypted backups

    Each day it saves an encrypted copy of your memory and notes to a folder you choose. Your passphrase restores it.

  • Works in aeroplane mode

    On a plane, off-grid or abroad without data, it still answers. Only downloads need a connection.

  • Talk instead of typing

    Speak a question and hear the answer, using your phone’s own offline speech.

How private is it?

What goes online

Downloads, and nothing else: AI models when you tap Download, the computer app when you install it, and the libraries you choose to add.

What never does

Your chats, memory, notes, photos, PDFs and voice. There’s no account, no analytics and no ads, and Android’s cloud backup is off.

  • Voice stays on the phone

    Aislet only uses the phone’s offline speech recognition. If a phone has none, there’s no voice typing rather than audio sent online.

  • Phone and computer talk directly

    Every request is signed with your pairing code, which never crosses the network. Replayed or altered requests are refused.

  • Check it yourself

    Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data: everything except downloads still works. Or read the security model and the attack tests.

Get Aislet

Android phone

Android 8 or later. A phone with 8 GB of memory runs the built-in AI best.

The app is about 60 MB. During set-up you can download an AI model for the phone (1.8 to 2.7 GB, best on Wi-Fi) or use your computer’s.

Download Aislet.apk

Open the file to install it, then follow the set-up. For automatic updates, add it to Obtainium. What’s new is in the release notes.

Check it’s the genuine app

The package is app.untethered.assistant, and the SHA-256 of its signing certificate is:

25:C6:D2:76:14:C5:E6:1B:20:58:6B:A2:62:64:E9:A8:8F:B0:49:95:20:17:AD:98:C2:14:4B:F6:90:1C:1E:9A

Computer

Windows 10 or 11: open PowerShell and run:

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/latczar/aislet/main/install/install.ps1 | iex

It adds Ollama and Python if they’re missing, picks an AI model to suit your graphics card, and puts Aislet in the Start menu.

Linux and Mac: install Ollama first, then open a terminal and run:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/latczar/aislet/main/install/install.sh | sh

Linux is tested on Ubuntu 24.04. The Mac version hasn’t been tested yet; if you try it, please say how it went.

To link them, open Settings, then Home, on the computer and scan the QR code with the phone’s camera. Both need to be on the same Wi-Fi.

Open source, so you can check for yourself.

Questions

What do I need?

An Android phone with Android 8 or later; 8 GB of memory is recommended for the AI that runs on the phone. The computer side is optional and runs on Windows 10 or 11, Linux or Mac, with Ollama. On Windows the installer adds Ollama for you.

Does it really work with no internet?

Yes. Once the AI model and any libraries are downloaded, chats, memory, notes, libraries and voice all work in aeroplane mode. At home, the phone and computer talk over your own Wi-Fi, not the internet.

How do I add Wikipedia or another library?

Download a .zim file from library.kiwix.org, such as Wikipedia or WikiMed, then turn on Libraries in Aislet’s settings and follow the steps there. If the Kiwix app already has your libraries, Aislet can move them across without making a second copy.

Which AI does it use?

Open models that run on your own devices: Qwen3.5 on the phone, and on the computer a model sized to your graphics card, through Ollama. Nothing is sent to an AI company.

What does it learn about me, and can I turn it off?

Facts you mention, like your job or what you’re working on, and how you like answers. They stay on your phone, and on your computer if you’ve paired them. Each one shows under the reply with Undo, and Memory lists them all to change or delete. If something changes, the old fact moves to No longer true. To stop it, switch off Learn from my chats in Memory.

Is it free?

Yes. No subscription, no ads, no account. It’s open source under the GPL, version 3 or later.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet: the phone app is Android only. The computer side runs on Windows, Linux and Mac.

Can it get things wrong?

Yes, like any AI. That’s why answers from a library name the article they came from, so you can read it yourself.
